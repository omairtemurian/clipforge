## The video

The video is stand-up comedy: a comedian tells jokes to an audience, and
the [LAUGH ...] markers show which jokes landed.

## Good clips

- Every clip follows setup -> punchline -> laugh: the setup paints the
  situation, the punchline turns it into a joke, and a [LAUGH ...] marker
  right after the punchline shows that the audience laughed. A run of jokes
  on one topic, each with its laugh, makes a good clip too.
- Start at the start of a sentence before the setup, so that a viewer who
  sees only the clip gets the whole joke. Never start at the punchline or
  in the middle of a sentence.
- End after the laugh marker, so that the laugh plays out: when the marker
  ends a line, add the laugh's seconds to the end time of that line, but do
  not go past the start of the next line. A clip whose last line is

      [02:10.0-02:14.5] S0: ... so now the dog knows my password. [LAUGH 2.5s 0.85]

  ends at 137.0 seconds (134.5 + 2.5).
- Prefer the jokes with the longest and strongest laughs. A punchline
  without a laugh marker probably did not land; do not end a clip on it.
- The first sentence makes the viewer curious about where the joke goes.
